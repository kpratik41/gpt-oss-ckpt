# Public-Source Search: Named Individuals, HKU, and China Military Connections

**Date:** 11 August 2026

## Scope and standard

Public web searches were performed for the four named individuals using combinations of their names with terms such as **"China military," "PLA," "People's Liberation Army," "National University of Defense Technology," "defense,"** and related institutional terms. A separate search was performed for **The University of Hong Kong (HKU)** and China/PLA/military links.

Only connections supported by identifiable public sources are reported. Name similarity, ordinary Chinese nationality/education, coauthorship, or general university affiliation is **not** treated as evidence of a military connection.

HIT was added by the U.S. Commerce Department to the Entity List in 2020 for activities the U.S. government said supported PLA programs, including seeking U.S. technology for Chinese missile programs. **Li's degree predates that 2020 listing and this does not establish that he personally participated in military work.**

- **Nan Huo:** No credible direct personal PLA/Chinese-military affiliation found.

These facts do **not** make HKU a military institution and do not establish a military connection for the four named people.

## HKU and China/PLA: relevant institutional connections

### A. PLA Hong Kong Garrison campus visit — 2010

HKU reported that approximately **80 members of the People's Liberation Army Hong Kong Garrison**, led by Deputy Commander Major General Wang Junli, visited HKU on **4 November 2010**. Activities included meetings with staff and students, a campus tour, a lecture, visits to laboratories/medical facilities, lunch, and a basketball match.

This is a documented **institutional engagement/exchange**, not evidence that HKU was conducting military research.

Source:
- HKU press release: https://www.hku.hk/press/press-releases/detail/6285.html

### B. HKU academic collaboration with Harbin Institute of Technology

HKU publicly documented a **joint Business Intelligence Laboratory with Harbin Institute of Technology** established in 2005, following prior collaboration and personnel exchanges.

HKU also advertised a **2025 winter-school exchange program at HIT** for HKU students.

Because HIT was subsequently placed on the U.S. Entity List in 2020 for the PLA-related reasons described above, these are relevant institutional links for diligence purposes. They remain **academic collaborations/exchanges** and do not, by themselves, demonstrate military work by HKU or the four named individuals.

Sources:
- HKU–HIT Joint Research Laboratory (2005): https://www.hku.hk/press/news_detail_5220.html
- HKU China Vision / HIT winter school (2025): https://www.chinavision.hku.hk/programmes/cv2526c015
- U.S. Federal Register on HIT (2020): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/06/05/2020-10869/addition-of-entities-to-the-entity-list-revision-of-certain-entries-on-the-entity-list

## Bottom line

The public-source review found **one material person-specific diligence item**: Jinyang Li's undergraduate degree from **Harbin Institute of Technology**, an institution later placed on the U.S. Entity List for PLA-related procurement/missile-program concerns. This is an **institutional association, not evidence of personal military involvement**.

For Reynold Cheng, Wenyu Du, and Nan Huo, I did not identify a credible direct military affiliation in the reviewed sources.

For HKU generally, there are documented contacts with the PLA Hong Kong Garrison and academic relationships with HIT, but the reviewed evidence does **not** support characterizing HKU itself—or the named individuals—as part of the Chinese military.

## Research limitations

- Public web search can miss Chinese-language, archived, paywalled, or non-indexed sources.
- Common Chinese names create a high risk of false attribution; identity matching must rely on education, employer, advisor, publications, and other corroborating details.
- A person's attendance at or collaboration with a university that has defense links should not be converted into an allegation about that person's conduct without person-specific evidence.
